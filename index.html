Polarization: j = 10 · 10^((η − η₁₀)/b) through the reported η at 10 mA/cm² with the reported Tafel slope b. It has no onset region, no capacitive current and no iR drop, and the paper scanned at 50 mV/s, faster than the 1 to 5 mV/s usual for OER. Nyquist: Z = Rs + 1/(1/Rct + Q(jω)ⁿ) from 0.01 Hz to 100 kHz using Table S3. The paper mentions two time constants but Table S3 lists one CPE, so only one loop is drawn. The unit of Q is not given in the supplement; S·sⁿ is assumed.

What the measurement does to the numbers

Physics model of one linear sweep through the kinetics of the chosen sample: j = 10 · 10^((E − E₁₀)/b) for the reaction, plus a capacitive current (capacitance × scan rate), plus the ohmic drop I · Ru, of which the chosen share is corrected. It answers: what would I read from this same sample with other settings? It does not predict a new sample.

Reported curve (what you read) Underlying kinetics of the sample

From the paper: Rs of Table S3 (used as Ru) and an electrode area of 0.25 cm². The paper does not say whether iR correction was applied, so 0 % is the default. The capacitance is not in the paper. The CPE of Table S3 corresponds to about 1 µF, which looks small to me for nickel foam and is worth asking the authors; 5 mF/cm² is an assumption. The Tafel fit uses the reported curve between 1 and 10 mA/cm². The paper's η and Tafel slope are taken here as the kinetics of the sample. If they already contain the iR drop, the real kinetics are better than shown and the shifts above are overestimated.

Configurational entropy against Cu

ΔS = −R Σ xᵢ ln xᵢ over the five metals, counting all cations together and taking the other four as equal. A spinel has two cation sublattices, so this is a simplification. The 1.5R limit for "high entropy" comes from alloys and is a convention for oxides; the lower limit of "medium entropy" is 1.0R in some sources and 0.69R in others. Circles use the ICP-MS compositions of Table S1; the line assumes the nominal ones.

How many replicates to tell two loadings apart?

Monte Carlo, 2000 simulated experiments per point: n electrodes per loading, equal-variance two-sided t-test at 5 %. The spread is your assumption. A round robin of the rotating-disk method found about 50 mV between laboratories for the same catalyst at 10 mA/cm², and less inside one laboratory, so measure your own by repeating one electrode.
